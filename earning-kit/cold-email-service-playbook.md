# Cold Email Service Playbook

Purpose: practical plan for getting the first paid local-business website client without calls as the main sales path.

Status: draft kit. Do not send outreach until the business email, sender name, and required footer details are ready.

## Goal

Cover the Codex cost with one small client.

- First target: 1 client at $250-$500.
- Good month: 2 clients at $300-$750.
- Do not chase app ideas, guru funnels, or vague Discord jobs while this test is active.

## Offer

Working offer:

```text
I help local service businesses turn more website visitors into quote requests by fixing contact paths, mobile CTAs, quote forms, and follow-up messages.
```

Short version:

```text
Website quote-flow fixes for local service businesses.
```

Plain client promise:

```text
I look at the path from landing on your site to requesting a quote, then fix the obvious leaks.
```

## First Niche

Start with one niche so every email sounds specific.

Recommended first niche: pressure washing.

Why:

- They sell by quote requests.
- Their sites often have weak mobile CTAs, old forms, thin proof, or slow follow-up.
- The service is easy to understand without industry expertise.
- Local owners usually care about calls, forms, and booked jobs more than abstract "AI."

Good backup niches:

- Junk removal
- Auto detailing
- Home cleaning
- Handyman/remodeling
- Landscaping

## Minimum Setup Before Sending

Do these before outreach:

- Pick sender name and business name.
- Use a business email on a domain if possible.
- Have a simple public site or sample audit link ready.
- Add a compliant footer with a real business mailing address or approved business address.
- Track unsubscribes manually.
- Send only targeted manual emails, not blasts.

Compliance notes:

- Use honest subject lines.
- Do not hide who sent the email.
- Include a clear way to opt out.
- Honor opt-outs.
- Do not buy scraped spam lists.
- Do not use Gmail account farms or limit bypassing.

## Prospect Rules

Good prospect:

- Local service business
- Has a website
- Has a quote/contact form or phone CTA
- Website has obvious friction
- Public email or contact form is available
- No franchise/corporate gatekeeping

Skip:

- No website
- Only Facebook page
- Huge company with marketing team
- Spammy company
- Medical/legal/financial claims-heavy site
- No public contact path
- Businesses requiring logins or private account access to review

## Audit Checklist

For each prospect, check:

- Is the main phone/quote button visible above the fold on mobile?
- Is there one clear action, or are there too many choices?
- Does the contact form ask for too much too early?
- Does the form explain response time?
- Is there a confirmation/next-step message after submitting?
- Are reviews close to the CTA?
- Is the service area obvious?
- Are before/after photos easy to see?
- Does the site feel slow or broken on mobile?
- Is there a missed-call or text-back path?

Score:

- 0 = not checked
- 1 = weak or missing
- 2 = okay
- 3 = strong

Pitch only when there are at least 2 obvious, fixable issues.

## Follow-Up

Wait 3-5 business days. Send one follow-up, then stop unless they reply.

```text
Hey [first name], just checking if you want me to send that quick site review.

I noticed [specific issue] and figured it may be worth a quick look before busy season.

If not, no worries.
```

## If They Reply Yes

```text
Sounds good. Send me the best site link if there is more than one.

I will keep it simple: 2-3 screen-recorded notes around the quote/contact path, then you can decide if any fixes are worth doing.
```

## Screen Recording Script

Keep it under 3 minutes.

Structure:

1. Open with the exact business name and page.
2. Say what a customer is trying to do.
3. Show issue 1 on mobile.
4. Show issue 2 near the contact/quote path.
5. Show one trust/review/follow-up gap.
6. End with a small paid next step.

Script:

```text
Hey [name], quick review for [business].

I looked at this like a customer trying to get a quote from my phone.

First thing I noticed is [issue 1]. I would fix that by [fix].

Second, [issue 2]. That matters because someone ready to book may not know what happens next.

Third, I would move or repeat [review/photo/service-area proof] closer to the quote path so the visitor has more confidence before reaching out.

If you want, I can clean this up as a small fixed-scope job. The first version would be [specific deliverable] for [$price], and I can do it async.
```

## Daily Workflow

90-minute version:

1. Find 10 pressure washing businesses in one city.
2. Skip the ones with strong sites.
3. Add 5 weak-but-real prospects to the tracker.
4. Write one specific issue for each.
5. Draft 5 personalized emails.
6. User reviews before sending.
7. Track replies and opt-outs.

Do not send more than 10-15 targeted emails/day at the start.

## First Week Plan

Day 1:

- Pick name/domain direction.
- Set up or confirm business email.
- Prepare compliant email footer.
- Build the prospect tracker.

Day 2:

- Find 25 pressure washing prospects.
- Pick the best 10.
- Draft personalized emails.

Day 3:

- Send first 5-10 manually after review.
- Prepare one sample screen-recording template.

Day 4-5:

- Send another 5-10.
- Record sample reviews for any "yes" replies.
- Quote the first small paid fix.

Day 6-7:

- Follow up with non-replies once.
- Improve the pitch based on replies.

## What Counts As A Win

Small wins:

- A real reply asking for the review.
- A prospect sending the best site/contact info.
- A prospect asking price.

Money win:

- Paid $99 audit.
- Paid $250-$400 quote-flow fix.

Stop/rethink if:

- 50 targeted emails get zero replies.
- Replies show the niche does not care.
- The offer sounds unclear after several conversations.
